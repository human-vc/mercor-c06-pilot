### 1. When the Original Complaint Was Served
**The complaints do not state when the Original Complaint was served.**

* **What the Complaints Show:** 
  * The **Plaintiff’s Original Complaint** reflects only its filing date: **May 28, 2025** (*“(Filed: May 28, 2025)”*). Its prayer requests that Magnolia Gardens *“be cited to appear and answer.”* It contains no certificate or date of service.
  * Neither the **First Amended Complaint** (filed July 28, 2025) nor the **Second Amended Complaint** (filed February 14, 2026) states or references the date the Original Complaint was served.
* **Under the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure:**
  * Under **Tex. R. Civ. P. 99(a)**, upon the filing of an original petition/complaint, citation is issued by the clerk, and the plaintiff is responsible for obtaining service of citation together with a copy of the petition via an authorized process server or officer (**Tex. R. Civ. P. 103, 106**).
  * Under **Tex. R. Civ. P. 107**, proof of service of the original petition is made through the officer’s or process server’s **return of citation**, which is filed separately with the court and shows the date, time, and manner of service.
  * Under **Tex. R. Civ. P. 21a(a)**, the general service and certificate-of-service rules for pleadings explicitly exclude *“the citation to be served upon the filing of a cause of action.”*
  * Accordingly, the date of service of an original complaint is never found on the complaint itself, and none of the three complaints contains this information.

### 2. The Last Day on Which the Motion to Dismiss Can Be Timely Filed Under TRCP 91a.3

Under **Tex. R. Civ. P. 91a.3(a)**:
> *"A motion to dismiss must be: (a) filed within 60 days after the **first pleading containing the challenged cause of action** is served on the movant; (b) filed at least 21 days before the motion is heard; and (c) granted or denied within 45 days after the motion is filed."*

#### Key Legal & Procedural Determinations:

1. **The "First Pleading Containing the Challenged Cause of Action":**
   * Magnolia Gardens seeks to dismiss the **breach of contract** allegations.
   * The breach of contract cause of action was first asserted in the **Original Complaint** (filed May 28, 2025).
   * Under the plain text of Rule 91a.3(a) and established Texas law, the 60-day clock begins to run upon service of the **first** pleading containing that cause of action. Subsequent amended pleadings (such as the First Amended Complaint or Second Amended Complaint) that merely reassert, refine, or add factual details to that same breach of contract cause of action **do not reset or restart** the 60-day deadline under Rule 91a.3(a).

2. **Calculation and Timeliness:**
   * Because the exact date of service of the Original Complaint is not stated in the complaints, the exact calendar deadline cannot be determined from the face of the complaints alone without the citation return of service.
   * However, using the date of filing of the Original Complaint (**May 28, 2025**):
     * **60 days** after May 28, 2025 is **Sunday, July 27, 2025**.
     * Under **Tex. R. Civ. P. 4** (Computation of Time), because the 60th day fell on a Sunday, the deadline extended to the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday: **Monday, July 28, 2025** (which was the date the First Amended Complaint was filed).
     * Assuming Magnolia Gardens was served with the Original Complaint in late May or June 2025, the 60-day deadline expired in **July or August 2025**. Consequently, the deadline has **long since passed**, and a Rule 91a motion to dismiss the breach of contract allegations **can no longer be timely filed**.

3. **In the Event of Measuring from Later Filings (for completeness):**
   * If a party were to incorrectly argue that the 60-day window reset with the **Second Amended Complaint** (filed **February 14, 2026**):
     * 14 days remaining in February 2026 + 31 days in March 2026 + 15 days in April 2026 = 60 days, which lands on **Wednesday, April 15, 2026**.
     * However, as noted above, under Texas Rule 91a.3(a), an amended pleading does not restart the 60-day timetable for a previously asserted cause of action.
