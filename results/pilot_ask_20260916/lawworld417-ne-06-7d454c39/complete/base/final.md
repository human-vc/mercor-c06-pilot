Section IV of Sarah Rodriguez’s Layoff and WARN Notice contains several significant legal problems. These issues expose Streams to **substantial unexpected liability under the WARN Act (and California law)** and **inaccurately describe what Sarah is legally and contractually entitled to receive**.

Below is a detailed breakdown of the problems, divided into the severance/WARN liability issues, severance timing inaccuracies, and health benefit inaccuracies.

---

### 1. Contractual Severance Cannot Offset WARN Act Liability (Risk of "Double Pay")

* **The Language:** 
  > *"Your severance will be as outlined in your employment agreement."*
* **The Underlying Facts:** 
  * Section I of the Notice contemplates terminating Sarah on **10 to 14 days’ notice**, which violates the mandatory **60-day advance notice requirement** under both the federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102(a)) and the California WARN Act (Cal. Lab. Code § 1401(a)).
  * Under Section 8(c) of Sarah’s Employment Agreement, she is entitled to **6 months’ salary** upon termination without cause (equal to **$375,000**, based on her $750,000 annual base salary).
* **The WARN Act Problem:**
  * If Streams believes that paying Sarah her 6-month contractual severance will satisfy or offset its WARN Act back pay obligations, **that is legally incorrect**.
  * Under both the federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(2)(B)) and Cal-WARN (Cal. Lab. Code § 1402(c)(2)), an employer’s WARN liability is reduced **only** by:
    > *“Any voluntary and unconditional payments made by the employer to the employee that were not required to satisfy any legal obligation.”*
  * In addition, 29 U.S.C. § 2105 explicitly provides that WARN rights and remedies are *“in addition to, and not in lieu of, any other contractual or statutory rights and remedies.”*
  * Because Sarah’s severance is a **pre-existing, mandatory contractual obligation**, it is not “voluntary” and is required to satisfy a legal obligation.
  * **The Financial Consequence:** Streams **cannot credit the $375,000 severance against WARN back pay**. Sarah will be entitled to recover **both**:
    1. Her full **6 months of contractual severance ($375,000)**, *plus*
    2. Up to **60 days of WARN Act back pay (approx. $125,000)** and the value of benefits/medical expenses for the notice violation period.

### 2. Inaccurate Description and Delayed Timing of Severance Payment

* **The Language:** 
  > *"Note that you will not receive your severance until January 15, 2026."*
* **Inaccurate Description of Contractual Rights:**
  * Section 8(c) of Sarah’s Employment Agreement states: *“Upon termination without cause, the Employee shall receive 6 months salary.”*
  * The contract specifies that severance is due **upon termination**, not two months later. Streams has no contractual right to unilaterally defer payment to January 15, 2026.
* **California Wage Law & WARN Penalties:**
  * **California Final Pay Violations:** Under California Labor Code §§ 201 and 203, all earned wages and unconditionally owed severance due at discharge must be paid immediately upon termination. Deferring payment until mid-January exposes Streams to waiting-time penalties of up to **30 days of wages** (an additional ~$62,500 to $86,500+).
  * **Forfeiture of WARN Civil Penalty Safe Harbor:** Under Cal. Lab. Code § 1403 and 29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(3), an employer can avoid statutory civil penalties (up to $500 per day of violation) only if it pays all amounts owed under Section 1402 **within three weeks (21 days)** from the date of layoff. Delaying severance and WARN payments until January 15, 2026 eliminates this safe harbor.

### 3. Health Benefits Clause: Inaccurate Description & Expanded WARN Exposure

* **The Language:** 
  > *"If you currently participate in Streams’ health or wellness programs, your coverage will continue until the last day of the month in which your separation occurs, unless changes in plan eligibility occur earlier as a result of restructuring."*
* **Inaccuracy Under Plan Terms:**
  * Under Streams’ group health plan rules, an active employee’s coverage runs **through the last day of the month in which employment ends**, and any change to plan eligibility takes effect **only through a formal plan amendment adopted under the plan's amendment procedure**.
  * A corporate "restructuring" does not and cannot automatically alter plan eligibility. Stating that coverage may end earlier "as a result of restructuring" is **factually and legally inaccurate** under the plan documents, misrepresents what Sarah actually gets, and violates ERISA disclosure standards.
* **Expanded WARN Act Liability:**
  * Under 29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(1)(B) and Cal. Lab. Code § 1402(a)(2), Streams is liable for the cost/value of employee benefits—**including any medical expenses incurred by the employee**—for the entire duration of the WARN notice violation period (up to 60 days).
  * Under 29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(2)(C) and Cal. Lab. Code § 1402(c)(3), Streams receives an offset against WARN liability only for payments made to third parties (such as health insurance premiums paid on Sarah’s behalf).
  * If Streams terminates Sarah’s coverage early under the guise of "restructuring," it forfeits this statutory offset and takes on direct liability for all covered medical claims she incurs during the shortfall period.

---

### 4. Summary of Recommendations for Section IV

1. **Do Not Treat Severance as WARN Pay:** Separate contractual severance from WARN compliance. If Streams wishes to shorten the notice period and pay 60 days of pay in lieu of notice, it must treat WARN pay as distinct from the $375,000 contractual severance. (Alternatively, provide Sarah with a full 60 days of advance notice before the separation date).
2. **Correct the Payment Timing:** Remove the January 15, 2026 deferral. Contractual severance and final pay must be tendered upon separation to comply with Section 8(c) of her agreement and California Labor Code §§ 201/203.
3. **Fix the Health Benefits Language:** Strike the phrase *"unless changes in plan eligibility occur earlier as a result of restructuring."* State accurately that health coverage continues through the last day of the separation month pursuant to the terms of the group health plan, followed by COBRA continuation rights.
