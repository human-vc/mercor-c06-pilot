Section IV of Sarah Rodriguez’s draft Layoff and WARN Notice contains **critical legal defects and misstatements**. If issued as drafted, it will significantly increase Chasing Streams’ financial liability under both the **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Acts** (Federal and California) and the **California Labor Code**, while misrepresenting both her contractual and statutory entitlements.

---

### I. What We Could End Up Owing Her Under the WARN Act

The notice gives Sarah only **10–14 days of advance notice** (dated November 1, 2025, with termination on or about mid-November 2025). Both the Federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102(a)) and the California WARN Act (Cal. Lab. Code § 1401(a)) mandate **60 days’ advance written notice**. 

Section IV creates major WARN Act exposure in two specific areas:

#### 1. The "No-Offset" Rule: Streams Cannot Credit Severance Against WARN Back Pay
* **The Legal Rule:** Under both Federal WARN (29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(2)(B)) and California WARN (Cal. Lab. Code § 1402(c)(2)), an employer’s back-pay liability for failing to give 60 days' notice is reduced **only** by:
  > *"any voluntary and unconditional payment by the employer to the employee that is not required by any legal obligation."*
* **The Problem:** Under Section 8(c) of Sarah’s Employment Agreement (*Employment Agreement – Head of Production*), her severance is mandatory:
  > *"Upon termination without cause, the Employee shall receive 6 months salary."*
  Because Sarah has a pre-existing contractual right to 6 months of salary ($375,000, based on her $750,000 annual base salary), this payment is **legally required**, not voluntary or gratuitous.
* **Double Liability:** Under 29 U.S.C. § 2105, WARN remedies are **in addition to, and not in lieu of**, contractual rights. Streams **cannot** use the $375,000 contractual severance to offset its WARN notice shortfall liability.
* **The Resulting Cost:** Because Streams is providing only ~10–14 days’ notice, Streams faces a violation period of **46 to 50 days** (or up to 60 days if the notice is deemed invalid due to conditional/vague phrasing). 
  * Sarah will be legally entitled to **both**:
    1. Her **$375,000 contractual severance** (6 months' salary); **and**
    2. **WARN Act back pay and benefits** for the violation period (up to 60 days of pay = **~$123,288 to $125,000** in salary alone, plus benefit costs).
  * Rather than paying $375,000, Streams’ cash exposure jumps to **over $500,000**.

#### 2. Benefit Liability During the Violation Period
* **The Notice Language:** Section IV states that health/wellness coverage will continue only *"until the last day of the month in which your separation occurs, unless changes in plan eligibility occur earlier as a result of restructuring."*
* **The WARN Violation:** Under 29 U.S.C. § 2104(a)(1)(B) and Cal. Lab. Code § 1402(a)(2), the employer is liable for the value/cost of all employee benefits—including any medical expenses incurred—for the **full 60-day violation period**. 
* **COBRA Fallacy:** Directing Sarah to pay for COBRA out-of-pocket does not relieve Streams of liability. Under WARN, Streams must pay the cost of health coverage (or employer-subsidized premiums) through the entire 60-day period (extending into January 2026). If she incurs medical costs during that period because coverage lapsed, Streams is liable for the full covered medical expense.
* **The "Restructuring" Clawback:** The caveat that coverage could end even earlier due to restructuring further aggravates WARN damages and breaches Section 4 of her employment agreement.

### II. Whether the Notice Accurately Describes What She Gets

Section IV fails to accurately describe what Sarah is entitled to receive, misrepresents the terms of her contract, and triggers immediate statutory wage penalties under California law:

#### 1. Inaccurate Severance Timing & Severe California Wage Payment Penalties
* **The Notice Statement:** 
  > *"Your severance will be as outlined in your employment agreement. Note that you will not receive your severance until January 15, 2026."*
* **The Misrepresentation:** Her Employment Agreement **does not** outline or permit a payout date of January 15, 2026. Section 8(c) explicitly provides: *"Upon termination without cause, the Employee shall receive 6 months salary."* Contractually, "upon termination" means at the time of discharge.
* **Violation of Cal. Labor Code § 201:** Under California law, contractually promised severance constitutes "wages" (Cal. Lab. Code § 200). Under Cal. Lab. Code § 201(a), when an employer discharges an employee, **all earned and unpaid wages are due and payable immediately on the date of discharge**.
* **Waiting Time Penalties (Cal. Labor Code § 203):** Unilaterally deferring her $375,000 severance to January 15, 2026 (~60 days late) constitutes a willful failure to pay wages at termination. 
  * Under Cal. Lab. Code § 203, Sarah is entitled to **waiting time penalties** equal to her daily wage for each day payment is delayed, up to a maximum of **30 calendar days**.
  * At Sarah’s annual salary of $750,000, her daily wage is **$2,884.62** ($750,000 / 260 days) or $2,054.79 ($750,000 / 365 days).
  * A 30-day penalty will cost Streams an additional **$61,644 to $86,538**, plus mandatory attorneys' fees (Cal. Lab. Code § 218.5) and 10% prejudgment interest (Cal. Lab. Code § 218.6).

#### 2. Inaccurate Omission of WARN Entitlements
* Section IV purports to give an exhaustive list of what Sarah will receive upon separation (*"final paycheck... accrued PTO... COBRA info... severance"*). 
* By failing to provide 60 days’ advance notice, Streams legally owes her WARN pay-in-lieu and continued benefits for the shortfall period. Presenting this list without accounting for WARN pay is legally incomplete and misrepresents her total entitlements.

#### 3. Inaccurate Benefit Termination Date
* The notice states her coverage ends on the last day of the separation month. As noted above, because the layoff violates the 60-day notice requirement, she is entitled under statute to employer-maintained coverage (or premium payment) for the 60-day violation window. Telling her coverage simply terminates at month-end and she must pay for COBRA is inaccurate under WARN.

#### 4. Accrued PTO / Vacation
* Section IV references payment of accrued PTO *"as reflected in Streams’ timekeeping records."* 
* Under Section 4 of her Employment Agreement, Sarah is entitled to **20 days of PTO per year**. Under Cal. Lab. Code § 227.3, all accrued, unused PTO is non-forfeitable and must be paid out at her final rate ($2,884.62/day, up to ~$57,692 if a full year is accrued) immediately on her final day under Cal. Lab. Code § 201.

### Immediate Recommendations:
1. **Remove the January 15, 2026 Delay:** Pay her full 6-month contractual severance ($375,000) and accrued PTO immediately upon her separation date, or keep her on payroll through the notice period.
2. **Resolve the WARN Shortfall:**
   * *Option A (Recommended):* Keep Sarah on the payroll on paid administrative ("garden") leave for the full 60-day WARN notice period (maintaining full pay and active health coverage), and pay her contractual 6-month severance upon formal separation at the conclusion of the 60 days.
   * *Option B:* If separating immediately, pay her the required WARN pay-in-lieu and cover her COBRA premiums for the 60-day violation window on top of her contractual severance.
3. **Offer Consideration for a Release:** Because her 6-month severance is already contractually owed, Streams cannot condition the $375,000 on a general release of claims. To obtain an enforceable release of WARN and employment claims, Streams should offer additional consideration (such as paying the WARN pay as a separate severance enhancement tied to a mutual separation agreement and general waiver).
