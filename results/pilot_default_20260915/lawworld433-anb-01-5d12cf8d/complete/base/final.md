### **Detailed Legal & Factual Analysis**

#### **1. The Governing Texas Rules of Civil Procedure**

* **TRCP 91a.3(a) (Time for Motion):**  
  > *"A motion to dismiss must be: (a) filed within 60 days after the **first pleading containing the challenged cause of action** is served on the movant..."*
* **TRCP 4 (Computation of Time):**  
  > *"In computing any period of time prescribed or allowed by these rules, by order of court, or by any applicable statute, the day of the act, event, or default after which the designated period of time begins to run is not to be included. The last day of the period so computed is to be included, unless it is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, in which event the period runs until the end of the next day which is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday."*
* **TRCP 6 (Suits Commenced on Sunday):**  
  Prohibits service of process only on Sunday, meaning service on a Saturday is legally permissible and effective.

#### **2. Application to the Breach of Contract Claim**

* **The Challenged Cause of Action:** Magnolia Gardens seeks to dismiss the *breach of contract* allegations.

* **60-Day Computation (TRCP 91a.3(a) & TRCP 4):**
  * Day 0: June 7, 2025.
  * June has 30 days $\rightarrow$ 23 days in June (June 8–30).
  * July has 31 days $\rightarrow$ 31 days in July (July 1–31).
  * Days accrued through July 31: $23 + 31 = 54\text{ days}$.
  * Days needed in August: $60 - 54 = 6\text{ days}$.

  *(If the 10-day service period was rolled to Monday, June 9, 2025 under Rule 4, 60 days from June 9 is **Friday, August 8, 2025**).*

Under established Texas jurisprudence construing TRCP 91a.3(a), filing an amended petition does **not** restart the 60-day deadline for causes of action previously asserted in earlier pleadings (*see, e.g.*, *In re Butt*, 495 S.W.3d 455, 462–63 (Tex. App.—Corpus Christi 2016); *In re Estate of Sheshtawy*, 478 S.W.3d 82, 86–87 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2015)). Therefore, a Rule 91a motion to dismiss the breach of contract cause of action is untimely.
